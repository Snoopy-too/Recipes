# Shallow-Fried Eggplant Agebitashi (Nasu no Agebitashi)

Silky, melt-in-your-mouth Japanese eggplant shallow-fried to lock in vibrant purple color and steeped in a savory dashi-shoyu-mirin bath with freshly grated ginger.

| Prep Time | Cook Time | Soaking Time | Servings |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 25 mins | 5 mins | 45 mins | 2–3 |

## Ingredients (Measured in Grams)

### Eggplant & Frying

- **Japanese Eggplants (Senryo Nasu):** 250–300 g (2 to 3 medium eggplants, halved lengthwise)
- **Neutral Frying Oil:** As needed to fill a small skillet 1.5–2 cm (1/2 to 3/4 inch) deep (canola, vegetable, or rice bran oil)

### Dashi-Shoyu Soaking Bath (4:1:1 Ratio)

- **Water:** 200 g
- **Dashi Powder (Hondashi):** 2 g
- **Shoyu (Soy Sauce):** 58 g
- **Mirin:** 58 g
- **Rice Vinegar (Optional):** 5 g (for a brighter finish)

### Garnish & Finish

- **Fresh Ginger (Shoga):** 15–20 g, freshly grated right before serving
- **Green Onions (Negi) or Katsuobushi (Optional):** For topping

## Step-by-Step Instructions

### 1. Score the Eggplant (Skip the Salt)

1. Trim the stems and slice each eggplant in half lengthwise.
2. Place each half **purple skin-side up** on the cutting board.
3. Using the tip of a sharp knife, score the purple skin in a fine diagonal or crisscross diamond pattern about **2–3 mm deep** (roughly one-third into the flesh), spaced about 5 mm apart.
4. Leave the white inner flesh completely flat and un-scored, and **do not salt** the eggplant.

### 2. Surface-Dehydrate (The Sweet Spot)

1. Arrange the scored eggplants **skin-side down** in the air fryer basket.
2. Run the **Dehydrate setting at 50°C–60°C (120°F–140°F) for 15 to 25 minutes**.
3. Check the cut surface: it should feel tacky, matte, and dry to the touch—not shriveled or leathery. *(Alternative: Let the cut eggplants sit uncovered on paper towels for 30 minutes.)*

### 3. Prepare the Soaking Bath

1. Combine the **200 g water**, **2 g dashi powder**, **58 g shoyu**, and **58 g mirin** (plus optional **5 g rice vinegar**) in a small saucepan or microwave-safe bowl.
2. Heat briefly until just simmering to dissolve the dashi granules and cook off the raw alcohol from the mirin.
3. Pour into a shallow, flat-bottomed dish and let cool to room temperature while you heat the frying oil.

### 4. Shallow-Fry in 1.5–2 cm of Oil

1. Pour neutral oil into your smallest suitable skillet to a depth of **1.5–2 cm (1/2 to 3/4 inch)** and heat to **175°C–180°C (350°F–360°F)**.
2. Working in uncrowded batches so the oil temperature stays high, lay the eggplants **purple skin-side down first**. Fry for **90 to 120 seconds** to lock in the bright purple pigment and cook through the score marks.
3. Flip **flesh-side down** and fry for another **60 to 90 seconds** until golden and tender.

### 5. Steep & Serve

1. Lift each piece from the oil, let excess oil drip off for 3–5 seconds, and drop the sizzling hot eggplant **directly into the room-temperature soaking bath, purple skin-side up (flesh-side down)**.
2. If the broth does not fully cover the tops, lay a sheet of kitchen paper towel directly over the eggplants (*otoshibuta* style) so it wicks the broth over the scored skin without submerging and dulling the purple color.
3. Let rest on the counter for **45 minutes** as the cooling eggplant pulls the savory broth deep into its flesh.
4. Transfer to a serving plate, spoon a little of the soaking broth over the top, and crown with **freshly grated ginger** right before serving.

## Chef's Notes

- **Why Skin-Side Up in the Bath:** Keeping the white flesh submerged maximizes broth absorption via capillary action, while keeping the purple skin above the dark soy sauce preserves its vibrant jewel tone.
- **No Salt Needed:** Japanese eggplants are naturally sweet without bitterness; pre-salting makes the finished dish overly salty once steeped in shoyu and turns the flesh spongy.
